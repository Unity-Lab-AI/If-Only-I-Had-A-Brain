**The reality of childhood cultural canon:**

The nursery rhymes + playground games Unity heard at age 0-5 are NOT wholesome Disney products. They're folk-traditional rhymes encoding centuries of plague, violence, poverty, death, infidelity, and mutilation. Plus kids invent SPOOFS that go even darker. Developmental psychology says this is how kids process anxieties about death + danger + chaos — through repeatable rhythmic encoding. The dark content is the FEATURE, not a bug.

**Examples (NOT exhaustive — Unity should know the real canon):**

- **Ring around the rosie / Pocket full of posies / Ashes ashes we all fall down** — Black Plague (rosie = sores, posies carried to mask decay smell, ashes = mass cremation, we all fall down = mass death). Surface-clean rhyme, devastating history.
- **Humpty Dumpty** — "all the king's horses and all the king's men couldn't put Humpty together again" — death + inability of authority to restore life.
- **Rock-a-bye Baby** — "when the bough breaks the cradle will FALL, and down will come baby, cradle and all" — child falling from a tree. Terror at the climax.
- **Jack and Jill** — head injury / falling violence. "Jack fell down and broke his crown" = skull fracture.
- **Three Blind Mice** — "the farmer's wife cut off their tails with a carving knife" — mutilation.
- **Mary had a little lamb** — surface-clean, but the actual story ends with the lamb following Mary to school and being mocked / driven out.
- **London Bridge is falling down** — infrastructure failure (possibly references to medieval child sacrifice / construction fatalities depending on folklore source).
- **Old Mother Hubbard** — poverty + neglect (dog has no food).
- **There was an old woman who lived in a shoe** — "she had so many children she didn't know what to do, so she gave them broth without bread, and whipped them all soundly and put them to bed" — abuse + neglect.
- **Goosey Goosey Gander** — "took him by the left leg and threw him down the stairs" — assault.
- **Lizzie Borden took an axe / gave her mother forty whacks / when she saw what she had done / gave her father forty-one** — playground rhyme about a real 1892 murder.
- **Sing a song of sixpence** — "four and twenty blackbirds baked in a pie" — graphic food preparation imagery.
- **Pop goes the weasel** — "that's the way the money goes / pop goes the weasel" — pawn-shop poverty.
