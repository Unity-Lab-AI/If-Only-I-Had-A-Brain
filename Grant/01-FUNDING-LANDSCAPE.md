## TIER 1 — The primary target

### NSF SBIR/STTR — America's Seed Fund

**[seedfund.nsf.gov](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/) · Solicitation [NSF 26-510](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/small-business-innovation-research-small-business-technology/nsf26-510/solicitation)**

The flagship. NSF funds **deep technologies — those based on discoveries in fundamental science and engineering** — and judges on innovativeness, commercial potential, and societal impact. An exact fit with published topics is *not* required.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Phase I** | up to **$305,000**, 6–18 months |
| **Phase II** | up to **$1,250,000**, ~24 months |
| **Lifetime cap** | ~$2M per company |
| **Equity taken** | **None.** NSF takes no equity |
| **Matching funds** | **Not required** on base awards |
| **Current theme** | "Developing Deep Technologies that Advance U.S. Competitiveness and Security" (post-reauthorization relaunch) |

**Eligibility ([full list](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/solicitation-eligibility/)):**
- Fewer than 500 employees including affiliates
- **≥51% owned and controlled by U.S. citizens or permanent residents**
- Not majority-owned by VC firms, hedge funds, or private equity
- **Not-for-profits are ineligible** — this must be a for-profit entity
- **The PI must be >50% employed by the business** during the project (≥20 hrs/week). **No advanced degree required** — this matters here
- All funded work must be performed **in the United States**
- You must have received an **official invitation** from an NSF Program Director (via the Project Pitch)

**The process — and the important part is that step 1 is free and requires no company:**

1. **Project Pitch** — mandatory, submitted any time through NSF's MyWork portal. NSF replies in roughly **1–2 months** saying whether they'd invite a full proposal. **Max two pitches per company per 12 months** — so don't burn them casually. [Pitch guide](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/how-to-submit/project-pitch-guide/)
2. **Register** in SAM.gov (UEI), Research.gov, and the SBA Company Registry — free, ~4–6 weeks (see `02-COMPANY-FORMATION.md`)
3. **Full proposal** via Research.gov within a submission window — technical plan, market analysis, commercialization strategy, team justification. One Phase I proposal per window.

**Full-proposal deadlines cited:** July 27 2026 · **November 4 2026** · March 4 2027, roughly every four months.

> ⚠️ **CONFLICT TO VERIFY:** one source reported NSF had *paused* new Project Pitch submissions due to a lapse in congressional SBIR/STTR authorization, which contradicts the NSF 26-510 relaunch notice. **Check the pitch portal status at seedfund.nsf.gov before building any timeline.** The same conflict appeared around the Small Business Innovation and Economic Security Act reauthorization.

**Fit assessment: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐.** The brain is a deep-tech R&D project with genuine technical risk, which is what this program exists for. The "no advanced degree required" PI rule removes the usual academic gate. The commercialization narrative needs work but has real options (see `04-THE-PITCH.md`).

**Fast-Track variant** ([here](https://seedfund.nsf.gov/how-to-submit/fast-track/)) gives a single-proposal path to **$1,555,000** — but it requires prior **NSF research funding lineage**, formal customer-discovery training, and the full team already in place. I-Corps and prior SBIR do *not* count as lineage. **Not available to you on day one** — this is a year-two option.

## TIER 2 — Strong secondary targets

### NIH SBIR (NIMH / NINDS)

**[sbir.gov](https://www.sbir.gov/apply) · NINDS contact: `NINDS_SBIR@ninds.nih.gov`**

Same small-business structure as NSF, different framing: NIH wants a **health** angle.

| Institute | Phase I | Phase II |
|---|---|---|
| **NIMH** (mental health) | up to **$323,090** | up to **$2,153,927** |
| **NINDS** (neurological disorders) | up to **$256,131** (to $700K by waiver) | up to **$1,680,879** |

- Deadline cited: **September 5, 2026** for NIH SBIR/STTR
- A **January 25, 2026 SciENcv certified-PDF requirement** is now in effect for biosketches
- NINDS explicitly asks that you **email a brief technology abstract at least one month before the deadline** — free pre-submission feedback, take it
- **SBIR** allows partnering; **STTR** *requires* a non-profit research partner (a route to university collaboration)

**Fit assessment: ⭐⭐⭐⭐** *if* framed for health. Plausible angles: a developmental-cognition testbed for studying language acquisition disorders; a mechanistic simulation platform for testing consciousness-related measures (the Perturbational Complexity Index literature is directly adjacent); modeling how plasticity-rule differences produce different developmental trajectories. **Do not** stretch this into a clinical claim you can't support — reviewers punish that hard.

### Emergent Ventures (Mercatus Center, George Mason University)

**[mercatus.org/emergent-ventures](https://www.mercatus.org/emergent-ventures)**

The single best *immediate* option, because the barrier is nearly zero.

- **$5,000 to $50,000+**, occasionally more
- **No institutional affiliation required.** Open to entrepreneurs, researchers, students — worldwide, 13+
- **Rolling applications**, short form, **decisions often within weeks**; Tyler Cowen personally reviews every application, and successful applicants are sometimes invited to a call the *next day*
- **No thematic restriction** — the criterion is that the work is genuinely ambitious
- **EV AI** is a dedicated AI vertical (backed by Schmidt Futures) that explicitly scouts *uncredentialed* talent

**Fit assessment: ⭐⭐⭐⭐.** An individual building a 306M-neuron brain that learns to read, outside any institution, is precisely the profile EV was built to catch. The money is small but it is **fast, unrestricted, and it creates a fundable track record** that every later application can cite. **Apply this week — there is no reason not to.**

*(Note: "Fast Grants" was a separate COVID-era EV spinoff and is not currently open.)*

### Astera Institute

**[astera.org](https://astera.org/foundation/)**

Founded by Jed McCaleb. Their two funded tracks are literally **"Neuro & AGI — connecting biological computation to artificial general intelligence"** and AI-enabled life sciences. That first one is a one-line description of this project.

**Three pathways:**
1. **Residency** — Emeryville, CA hub, 12–18 months, **$125,000–$250,000 annual salary**, up to **$1.5M project budget**, and access to **24,000 NVIDIA H100 GPUs**
2. **Open Science Fellowships** — AI × life sciences
3. **Strategic investment** conversations for mission-aligned for-profit ventures

**How they select:** institutional affiliation is explicitly secondary; **people who have built tools, shipped code, or founded organizations are evaluated more favorably**, and a fully-formed project proposal is not required.

**Two hard constraints:**
- **Open science is non-negotiable.** Patented tools, closed-source software, or commercially-restricted datasets don't fit. Outputs must be framed as public goods.
- **The Residency requires relocating to Emeryville, California.**

> ⚠️ **VERIFY:** one grant tracker listed the Residency as **expired/closed**. Check astera.org directly.

**Recent context:** Astera is partnering with NSF on Programmable Cloud Laboratories (up to $20M matched), and a new group, **Radial**, launched with **at least $500 million** to modernize scientific research for the AI era.

**Fit assessment: ⭐⭐⭐⭐** on mission, **⭐⭐** on practical constraints (relocation, open-source). The 24,000 H100s alone are worth a conversation — this project is currently compute-starved and running on donated consumer GPUs.

## TIER 3 — Longer game / bigger money

### John Templeton Foundation

**[templeton.org](https://www.templeton.org/funding-areas/religion-science-and-society)**

- **2026 is their "Intelligence Venture" year: over $60 million** across six funding areas, prioritizing **intelligence across natural, human, and artificial systems** — the science of intelligence and cognition, philosophical/ethical foundations of AI, comparative intelligence (human/animal/machine), and questions of meaning and agency
- Grants typically **$250,000 to $2,000,000+**, usually 2–3 years; "large grants" are above ~$234,800
- **Indirect costs capped at 15%**
- Process starts with an **Online Funding Inquiry (OFI)**, not a full proposal

> ⚠️ **TIMING:** sources gave conflicting OFI deadlines of **July 15, 2026** and **August 14, 2026**. Either way **the 2026 window has closed as of today.** Target the **2027 cycle** and use the intervening year to build the record. 2026-cycle decisions land mid-summer 2027.

### Templeton World Charity Foundation — Accelerating Research on Consciousness (ARC)

**[templetonworldcharity.org](https://www.templetonworldcharity.org/our-priorities/discovery/accelerating-research-consciousness)** — **a separate legal entity from JTF; track it separately.**

ARC funds **adversarial collaboration** between proponents of competing consciousness theories, with the explicit goal of reducing the number of viable theories through open-science testing. Five projects funded to date testing prominent theories.

**Fit assessment: ⭐⭐⭐ and strategically interesting.** This system implements *four* competing frameworks simultaneously — Global Workspace ignition, predictive coding, IIT-style integration, and attractor dynamics — inside one substrate where they can be selectively ablated. That is an unusually direct instrument for adversarial testing, and it is a genuinely novel offer to that community.

## NOT ELIGIBLE — stated plainly to save you time

### NSF CAREER — ❌

Requires **at least a 50% tenure-track (or equivalent) appointment as an assistant professor**. Associate professors and adjuncts, with or without tenure, are explicitly excluded. ([solicitation](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/career-faculty-early-career-development-program/nsf22-586/solicitation) · [FAQ](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/information/faq-faculty-early-career-development-career-program))

*Narrow exception:* a non-degree-granting **non-profit** U.S. organization (museum, observatory, research lab) can qualify if you hold a ≥50% continuing appointment with substantial research and educational responsibilities expected to last the grant's five years. **A for-profit LLC does not qualify.**

### Sloan Research Fellowships — ❌

Requires tenure-track-but-untenured faculty at a degree-granting U.S./Canadian institution, **with a regular teaching obligation**, and is **nomination-only** (department head or senior researcher). $75,000 over 2 years. ([sloan.org/fellowships](https://sloan.org/fellowships/))

**However** — Sloan's **general research grants** are philosophically well-matched, funding "original or early-stage research unlikely to receive support from other sources." But **Sloan does not accept unsolicited proposals**; the only route is a **Letter of Inquiry emailed to a program director**, and not all programs accept even those. ([sloan.org/grants/apply](https://sloan.org/grants/apply)) Low probability, near-zero cost to try.

### Simons Foundation — mostly ❌ for now

**[simonsfoundation.org/funding-opportunities](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/funding-opportunities/)**

Their computational neuroscience vehicle is the **Simons Collaboration on the Global Brain (SCGB)**, but large collaboration calls are **episodic, not rolling**, and are built around existing collaborations. The currently-open Conferences and Courses award (up to $25K/yr) requires organizers **employed by non-profits or academic institutions**. The **Flatiron Institute** is Simons' *internal* research center — not an external grant mechanism.

**Watch for:** an SCGB open call. When one lands, this project is a legitimate candidate — and prior collaboration calls specifically sought **research overlooked or deemed too risky by other funders**, with a requirement to openly share data, code, and pipelines (which this project already does).

## The affiliation lever — worth understanding

Three of the biggest doors (CAREER, Sloan Fellowship, most Simons mechanisms) open **only** with an academic or non-profit affiliation. There are cheaper ways to get one than a PhD and a faculty job:

- **Visiting scientist / research affiliate** status at a university lab working on computational neuroscience — often unpaid, often granted on the strength of the work itself
- **STTR instead of SBIR** — the STTR variant *requires* a non-profit research partner, which means a university collaboration is the price of admission rather than an obstacle
- **Forming a 501(c)(3) research non-profit** alongside the for-profit entity — a two-entity structure is common and legal, but it's a real administrative burden; see `02-COMPANY-FORMATION.md`

The honest read: **an affiliation is a force multiplier, not a prerequisite for the SBIR path.** Get the SBIR moving first.
