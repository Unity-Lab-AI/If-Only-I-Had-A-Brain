## The one-paragraph answer

**The realistic money for this project is federal SBIR — specifically NSF's America's Seed Fund: up to $305,000 for Phase I, non-dilutive, no equity taken, no matching funds required.** It requires a U.S. small business, which means forming an entity (an LLC is fine), getting an EIN, and completing three free federal registrations that take about 4–6 weeks end to end. The cheapest possible first move costs **nothing and requires no company at all**: NSF's mandatory **Project Pitch** is a short free submission that NSF answers in about 1–2 months telling you whether they'd invite a full proposal. In parallel, **Emergent Ventures** takes rolling applications from unaffiliated individuals with no institution required and decisions in weeks. Those two are the immediate moves. Everything else is a longer game.

## The strategic read

Three things about this project matter for funding, and they cut in different directions:

**1. The technical story is genuinely fundable.** A 306-million-neuron simulation running Rulkov map neurons, Oja plasticity, Hopfield attractor memory, Kuramoto coupling, and a Global-Workspace ignition gate — learning language developmentally instead of from a text corpus — is exactly the "deep technology based on discoveries in fundamental science and engineering" that NSF SBIR was written for. It carries real technical risk, which is a *requirement*, not a liability. The theory paper at `docs/THEORY-PAPER.md` is already most of a technical narrative.

**2. The distributed-compute architecture is a second, independently fundable story.** Volunteer GPU donation running biological-scale neural simulation, with a measured wire protocol and honest backpressure governance, is a systems contribution that stands on its own — and it is more obviously commercial than the cognition work.

**3. The persona layer needs deliberate handling.** This is addressed candidly in `05-RISKS-AND-HONEST-NOTES.md`. The short version: the science is fundable, the adult-content dimension is not something a federal reviewer will fund, and the two are separable in how you present the work without misrepresenting anything.
